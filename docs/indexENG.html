1. Background of the Event

In recent years, rapid changes in work environments, climate, and technology have posed unprecedented challenges to Occupational and Environmental Health and Safety (OEHS). This symposium was organized in response to emerging occupational health concerns and strategic directions in Taiwan, with reference to international professional organizations such as AIHA, BOHS, and IOHA.

Key international trends include:

  1. Climate Change and Extreme Exposure – Rising global temperatures increase heat injury risks for outdoor and high-temperature workers. AIHA, BOHS, and IOHA are advancing heat stress assessment and prevention. Climate change also brings wildfire-related air pollution and biological hazards like mold and infectious agents.

  2. Sustainability and Emerging Chemical Hazards – Engineered stone processing has caused severe accelerated silicosis, prompting BOHS to advocate stronger controls. Emerging threats such as PFAS and flame retardants (e.g., TBBPA) are gaining attention, alongside smart PPE and big data predictive analytics.

  3. Total Worker Health (TWH) – Advocated by NIOSH and prioritized by AIHA, TWH expands occupational health beyond traditional hazards to include non-traditional workers (gig economy, remote work), excessive working hours, psychosocial stress, and ergonomic injuries (e.g., HAVS).

  4. AI and Smart Monitoring – AI, sensors, and big data enable real-time monitoring of hazardous gases and noise, but raise ethical issues around worker privacy and IH data standardization.

  5. Next-Generation Occupational Health Strategies – IOHA estimates global demand for qualified OEHS professionals at ~44,000 versus only ~20,000 available. AIHA’s 2026 Strategy and BOHS’s 2026–2030 Strategy prioritize “Prevention-First” and capacity building in emerging regions. Their shared direction includes: strengthening prevention-first science and practice (e.g., PGPs for thermal stress and lithium battery fire exposure); expanding professional influence and public policy advocacy; enhancing transnational leadership and local OEHS capacity; and advancing organizational excellence, digital transformation, and agile governance.

  6. Domestic Research and Development – Taiwan faces challenges linked to international trends, including climate change, new chemical and engineered stone dust hazards, and diverse labor patterns. Learning from AIHA, BOHS, and IOHA can help Taiwan align with international research and policy directions. International exchange also enhances Taiwan’s visibility and partnerships in the global OEHS field.

This symposium invites experts from industry, government, academia, and research institutions to share domestic and international research findings, policy experiences, and practical perspectives, serving as a reference for Taiwan’s future research, policy planning, and implementation.
